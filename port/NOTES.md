# The port's notes

## The package

`ac-import <source> <package> <cache>` (`importer/`) writes the package the game reads from boot to Town's first frame and its sound, importer `ac`, version 4. Each entry is a whole disc file, at the game's own name for it, or made from one:

| Entry | Recipe | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| `COPYDATE` | `copy` 1 | none: text |
| `static.str` | `copy` 1 | none: strings |
| `forest_1st.arc` | `archive` 2 | the directory, and the files below |
| `forest_2nd.arc` | `archive` 3 | the directory, and the files below |
| `famicom.arc` | `archive` 1 | the directory only |
| `audiorom.img` | `audiorom` 1 | the banks laid out for the host ([Sound](#sound)) |
| `audiorom.tbl` | `audiorom-table` 1 | made from `audiorom.img`: the host image's bank and data tables |

Each archive stays whole. Its directory is laid out again at the host's sizes and in the host's byte order, and its file data keeps the console's offsets, names and order (sonarlib's `docs/porting-model.md`, Import).

Version 3 (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 step 11c) converts the banners' and icons' headers in `forest_2nd.arc`. Version 4 (step 9f0) converts `forest_1st.arc`'s other message tables and `forest_2nd.arc`'s `fgnpcdata.bin`, house walls and floors. `ac_port` refuses a package from before the current version, saying to import the disc again: an old package would hand `mCD_set_bti_data` the headers' offsets, and the message code the tables' offsets, in the console's order. Only the importer's version makes the port refuse a package; an archive's recipe version keys the import cache alone.

## The archives

Converted files are converted in place, at their offsets and sizes.

- **`forest_1st.arc`:**
  - converted:
    - `fgdata.bin`, Town's item layers, through `ac.schema`'s `FgData`;
    - the ten message tables, `string_data_table.bin` and the `mail`, `maila`, `mailb`, `mailc`, `ps`, `psz`, `select`, `super` and `superz` `_data_table.bin`, their `u32` words swapped. `mMsg_Get_BodyParam` reads each the same way, its offsets and sizes as native words (`m_msg_main.c_inc:283-298`). Unconverted, the choice menus show "iiii", and letters read from the wrong place or are not loaded;
    - `pallet_boy.bin`, its palettes swapped;
    - `face_boy.bin`, its palettes swapped and its texels kept;
  - kept as the console's: the `*_data.bin` texts, read byte by byte, and `tex_boy.bin`'s texels.
- **`forest_2nd.arc`:**
  - converted:
    - `fgnpcdata.bin`, the villagers' house rooms, 510 records, through `ac.schema`'s `FgData` as `fgdata.bin`. `mFM_SortFGData` indexes a list of 536 by each record's `fg_id` with no upper bound (`m_field_make.c:84-104`), so unconverted ids wrote past it into the heap;
    - `message_data_table.bin`, its `u32` words swapped;
    - `player_room_wall.bin` and `player_room_floor.bin`, the house walls and floors, 71 records of 0x1020 bytes and 71 of 0x2020 (kinds `room_wall` and `room_floor`): each record's 0x20-byte palette at its start swapped as 16 `u16` entries, and its two or four 64 × 64 4-bit images kept. The palette's buffer also takes a player's own design from a table in the host's order (`mNW_CopyOriginalPalette`), so the file's palettes must be the host's too;
    - the 41 `.bti` images, the banners and icons the card files take, as `jsyswrap.cpp:412-452` names them: each one's `CResTIMG` header through `ac.schema`'s `CResTIMG`, and its texels and palette kept, as the card holds them in the console's order. `mCD_set_bti_data` reads the header's offsets natively and copies what follows as it is (`m_card_bti.c_inc`);
  - kept as the console's:
    - `my_original.bin` and `needlework.bin`, texels; the e-Reader program after them is copied as bytes and written with `putLEWord` (`m_eappli.c:93-110`);
    - `npc_name_str_table.bin` and `message_data.bin`, text;
    - the island's GBA files, `d_obj_npc_stock_sch.bin`, `d_obj_npc_stock_scl.bin` and `d_bg_island_sch.bin`. They go only to a GBA, and `mISL_int` and `mISL_short` reverse each word as they load and store it (`m_island.c:173-191`), which gives the same bytes on either host; converting them would break what the GBA receives.
- **`famicom.arc`:** none of its contents is converted. Its members are Yaz0, under a root that is not a `DATA` node, so converting them would mean expanding them and laying the archive out anew, and a converted archive keeps every offset. Three sites read a `ResTIMG` header from its `.bti.szs` card images, each from the fresh copy a fetch expands: `famikon.bti.szs` in `SetupInternalCommentImage`, on every town save, and `newdisk_system.bti.szs` and `newdisk.bti.szs` in `SetupExternCommentImage`, reached only through the Famicom emulator, which declines to start on the port. `famicom.cpp` converts each header it fetches to the host's order ([The edits on the path](#the-edits-on-the-path)). Nothing else in the archive is read while the emulator does not start.

## The program

`ac_port` (`adapter/ac_port.c`) runs the game on sonarlib. By default it runs headless, in deterministic mode, from boot to play's first frame (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 steps 6f, 6g and 6h); with `--window` it shows the game in a window in real time (step 6k) and plays its sound (step 6l). `--goldens` and `--measure` run headless on the GPU sink with no window (steps 6n and 7b), `--replay` plays the game's controllers from an input recording, headless (step 9f1), and `--window --record` records a window's input into one (step 9f2b), with the player's cards (step 9f2c). In every deterministic run, `--state-hashes` writes a hash of the game's state at each tick boundary and `--dump` dumps it at a tick (step 9f3b, [State hashes](#state-hashes)). Each frame runs from `JW_BeginFrame` to `JW_EndFrame`: its clear, J2D's draws and the text are recorded into it by sonarlib's `kits/jsys` and emu64's tasks by sonarlib's `kits/emu64`, and it is submitted to the render module. `ac_port` links no GX front end (step 10e). Headless that is the null sink, and the run opens no window and reads no device; the game's sound is mixed on sonarlib's null audio sink, counted and not heard. `ac_port_reference`, a test program of the same build, is `ac_port` with the console's emu64 to compare and measure the kit against, under `--diff` and `--reference` (step 10a, [The reference's program](#the-references-program)).

### The threads

The process's main thread starts sonarlib, the render module, emu64's kit, fs and os. It then creates and starts a game thread named `main` at priority 0, which starts the runtime and calls the game's `main`, so the game's main thread is the runtime's tick thread. `OSInit` binds it as the SDK's default thread. Headless, the process's main thread joins it and the run ends with `_Exit`. Under `--window` the process's main thread stays outside the game's threads and presents (below). In normal mode, while the tick thread waits in os, sonarlib's idle driver fires each retrace on time.

### The run

`ac_port` mounts the package as `game:`, starts sonarlib's os and runtime in deterministic mode at NTSC's video rate, from a fixed calendar (2001-12-14 12:00:00), and calls the game's `main`. When the game has submitted play's first frame it prints the frame line and exits 0:

```
ac_port: play tick <t> retrace <r> gx <bytes> dvd <reads> <bytes> hash <h> instances <n> compiles <n>
    holes <vertex>/<list> cpu <n> refused <n> views <n> invalid <n> problems <n> overflows <n> unowned <n>
    strays <n> gx-errors <n> unmapped <n> outside <n> nested <n> refused-frames <n> audio <n> loud <n> dsp <n>
    sound <h>
```

on one line: the ticks and retraces run, the GX bytes written outside display lists, the DVD reads and their bytes; the hash of the frame submitted last; from emu64's kit, the instances its last task recorded, its compiles, the vertex and list holes it replayed, its draws worked out on the CPU, its refusals and its views past the render module's 256; from the render module, its invalid frames, problems and overflows; the actors the glue drew without an owner, being outside main memory and every table of static actors ([The glue](#the-glue)); the kit's strays, the part markers outside every owner or out of range, the closes with nothing open and the owners and parts left open at a task's end; and from the GX glue, its errors (the bytes it dropped, GX written outside a display list, and under the reference's flags the hand-overs its front ends failed), the pointers it could not map to a GX address, emu64's tasks with no frame open, the frame begins and ends out of order, and the frames the render module refused; then, from sonarlib's audio mix statistics (`sl_audio_get_mix_stats`), the output frames mixed, those with a sample at 1/256 of full scale or above, and the hash of every output sample; and the DSP glue's frames whose rendered channels hold a sample at 1/256 of full scale or above ([Boot sound](#boot-sound)). If `n` ticks pass first (3600 by default) it prints the same fields as `ac_port: limit …` and exits 1. Wrong arguments exit 2, and a failure before the game starts exits 1, a package from another importer version saying to import the disc again. `ac_port_reference --reference` draws with the console's emu64 instead of the kit ([The reference's program](#the-references-program), [Measured runs](#measured-runs)), and names the line `reference`. `--validate` creates the kit with `SL_EMU64_VALIDATE`, so every use of a program hashes the ranges it read again, and a static list the game rewrote is compiled again rather than drawn stale; its line must match a plain run's. The test `ac_port_play` imports the disc and runs `ac_port` twice and once with `--validate`, and all three must print the same line, with instances, a hash, and no invalid frame, view past 256 or overflow, the GX glue's five counts all 0, audio frames above 0 and `dsp` above 0: the boot jingle sounded. Headless runs pace the mix by alarms in deterministic mode, so the audio fields match too. The title's music starts after play's first frame, so the loud frames here are the jingle's.

On `GAFE01_00` the three runs print, as the reference line:

```

### Replays

`--replay <recording>` plays the game's controllers from an input recording, headless (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 step 9f1). sonarlib's `sl-replay`, built with the port at `port/build/sonarlib/tools/sl-replay`, builds a recording from a script, a text file of a controller's connections, presses and moves, each at a retrace (sonarlib's `docs/platform.md`, Replay scripts):

```sh
port/build/sonarlib/tools/sl-replay build port/tests/town_replay.txt town.slir
port/build/ac_port --package ac.pkg --replay town.slir
```

The run is a plain one ([The run](#the-run)), deterministic from the same calendar unless the recording holds one, but:

- `main` reads the recording whole before sonarlib starts, and starts sonarlib's input module with no backend (`sl_input_init(NULL)`): four GameCube ports, each disconnected until the recording connects it. After the package's mount and before os starts it binds os's system settings (`sl_os_settings_bind`) and starts the replay (`sl_input_replay_start`), which checks the recording's packages against the one mounted and puts its settings in effect at once, so os starts with them and the recording holds every partition from tick 0. os starts at the recording's calendar, or at the headless one when it holds none, as `sl-replay`'s recordings do, and `init_rnd`'s seed is the one the port's data gives, when the recording holds it ([Recording](#recording)). The game reads the retrace view: `padmgr` reads the pads at each retrace (`PADRead`, on sonarlib's `kits/pad`), and the game takes them at its next frame, so Start at retrace 1000 reaches it at 1002.
- Play's first frame prints the play line and the run goes on. When the replay has ended, at the tick after the recording's last, the retrace callback that checks the tick limit prints the same fields as `ac_port: replay …`, then the saves line when the run has a store ([Saves in recordings](#saves-in-recordings)), and exits 0. The line's tick counts the ticks begun, so it is the recording's ticks + 1. Each line ends ` scene <n>`, the scene the game is in (`Save_Get(scene_no)`); other runs' lines name none.
- No tick limit applies unless `--ticks` gives one, and then the `limit` line ends the run at it with exit 1, as in a plain run.
- A recording that carries saves, even none, replays on a memory store seeded from them, its slots as it recorded them ([Saves in recordings](#saves-in-recordings)). One that carries none, as `sl-replay`'s do, has no store and no card in either slot.
- A recording that cannot be read, is not one this library plays, has other than four ports, holds another port's or another version's data, or was recorded with another package exits 1 with a message; the last says to import the disc it was recorded from. `--replay` given twice, or with `--goldens`, `--reference` or `--record`, or with `--diff` in a window, exits 2 with the usage, as does `--qrand-seed` with a recording that holds the port's data, which gives the seed. `--validate`, `--ticks` and `--window` go with it, `--diff` headless ([The emu64 differential test](#the-emu64-differential-test)), `--measure` with `--ticks` ([Measured runs](#measured-runs)), and `--qrand-seed` with a recording that holds no port's data.

In a window (step 9f2a), `--window --replay <recording>` plays the recording as headless does, frame for frame, while the window shows it at the game's speed. The run starts as the headless one does and as a window run does otherwise ([The window](#the-window)):

- The runtime and os are deterministic, from the recording's calendar, else the headless one, 2001-12-14 12:00, and the runtime is paced (`sl_runtime_desc.paced`): each move of virtual time waits for the host to reach its time on the run's schedule, so the game sees what the headless run gives it, at the game's speed. `--unpaced` runs it as fast as it can, and is a usage error without `--window` and either `--replay` or `--record`.
- For a recording that carries no saves, no save store and no card bound to a slot, so both slots are empty, as headless, and the cards' settings are not registered; one that carries saves has the headless replay's memory store and its cards. os's settings are bound from the settings file as in any window run, and the replay puts the recording's in effect over them until its end, when the player's return. A change the game makes to them in the replay acts in os at once and is stored nowhere, as the recording gives the same change at the same tick boundary, so the settings file keeps the player's (sonarlib's `include/sonar/os.h`). A `--set` naming one of the cards' settings names a setting nothing registers, and one naming a setting applied at the tick boundary, as os's are, names one the replay takes from its recording: each is a usage error. The overlay's page shows no saves directory and says the slots are empty. The window's, the audio's and the input's settings, the overlay, `--user-dir` and `--set` act as in any window run.
- The game's sound plays on the audio device with the console's rate, 32029 Hz, as its output rate, so the line's audio fields are the headless run's; the platform resamples to the device.
- The window's input starts once, with its bindings; the replay holds the ports, so the keyboard and gamepads wait behind it.
- The retrace callback is registered as headless. At the replay's end it prints the replay line once, and the saves line when it has a store, and tells the present loop, which ends: the settings are saved, the window line is printed, with the slots' cards, and the run exits 0. `--ticks`, or closing the window, ends it as any window run.

The frames are drawn on the GPU sink, which hashes them as the null sink does, so the play and replay lines are the headless run's, byte for byte. Every other window run stays in real time.

The test `ac_port_window_replay` builds the town replay and `tests/title_replay.txt`, the title's Start to tick 1200, scene 28 at 1060, and runs each headless and in a window, offscreen as `ac_port_window`, with `--user-dir` under its own directory. The town replay's window run is `--unpaced`: it must exit 0, its play line and its one replay line must be the headless run's, and its window line must follow them and end with `cards - - target 640x480`. It takes about 70 seconds on lavapipe, against about 10 headless. The title replay's window run is paced: it must exit 0 with the headless run's replay line, in scene 28, take no less than 20 seconds, 1200 ticks at 60000/1001 Hz, and blend presents, `blended` above 0; it takes about 21. It runs again paced with `--set sonar.video.interpolation=false`, and must exit 0 with the headless run's lines, `blended 0` and no more frames than ticks, about 1200 for 1201, and leave no `interpolation` in the settings file, as the `--set` lasted for its run; it takes about 21 more. `--unpaced` without `--replay`, `--unpaced` without `--window`, and a window replay with `--set sonar.system.progressive_scan=true` must each exit 2. It is skipped without a disc or lavapipe.

### Recording

`--window --record <recording>` records the window's input from tick 0 to `--ticks`'s limit or the window's close into a recording that `--replay` plays (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 steps 9f2b and 9f2c):

```sh
port/build/ac_port --package ac.pkg --window --ticks 1200 --record title.slir
port/build/ac_port --package ac.pkg --replay title.slir
port/build/sonarlib/tools/sl-replay show title.slir
```

The run is a window run ([The window](#the-window)), but:

- The runtime and os are deterministic, os from the host's calendar at the start, or `--date`'s ([Saves in recordings](#saves-in-recordings)), and the runtime is paced as a window replay's ([Replays](#replays)): the game runs at its speed on the window's input, and `--unpaced` runs it as fast as it can. The recording holds the calendar, so each replay starts on the same date and runs frame for frame as the recording did.
- The settings file is loaded, each `--set` taken over it and os's system settings bound as in any window run, so the game boots with the player's, and the recording holds the settings in effect at its start and each change at the tick boundary it applies at. A change from the overlay or a device script's `set` step is stored as a player's change is, so it reaches the settings file; a `--set` lasts for the run, as always.
- The player's save store is opened and the cards' settings bound as in any window run, so the game saves to the player's cards, and the recording carries the store's saves as they were at its start ([Saves in recordings](#saves-in-recordings)). The game's sound plays at 32029 Hz, as a window replay's.
- `--input-script <script>` loads a device script (sonarlib's `docs/platform.md`, Device scripts) after the binds, so its `set` steps name registered settings, and before the game thread starts: its devices take the ports, the host's keyboard and gamepads take none, and they reach the game through the window's devices, bindings and the GameCube profile as the host's would. A line the library refuses prints `ac_port: <script>:<line>: <why>` and exits 1.
- The file is opened just before the game thread starts, and the game thread starts the recording (`sl_input_record_start`) before the runtime, with the port's data: a version byte, 1, whether `--qrand-seed` was given and its seed, a `u32` little-endian, which a replay takes as its seed. The recording's bytes are written at each tick boundary.
- At the retrace where the runtime has begun the limit's ticks, or the first after the window closed, by the player or a device script's `close`, the retrace callback stops the recording (`sl_input_record_stop`), which ends at that tick's boundary. Until then a closed window goes on presenting. One retrace later, where its replays end, it closes the file and prints the same fields as `ac_port: record …`, at tick limit + 1 or the close's tick + 1, with the scene, then the saves line, and the present loop ends: the settings are saved, the window line is printed, with the slots' cards, and the run exits 0, or 1 when a write or the close failed, printing why. When the recording has not ended 5 seconds after the close, the run prints `ac_port: <recording>: the recording did not end after the window closed` and exits 1.
- `--record` without `--window`, given twice or with `--replay`, `--input-script` without `--record`, and `--date` without `--record`, given twice or with a date that is not one or is before 2000, each exit 2 with the usage and write no recording.

Its replays print the record run's play line, its record line, named `replay`, and its saves line, headless and in a window. A recording's lines depend on the date os starts at, the host's unless `--date` gives one, so without it they differ from run to run, and from a headless run's. An unpaced recording ended by a close ends at the first retrace after the window takes the quit, which depends on the host's timing, so its end tick can differ from run to run; its replays end where it did.

### Saves in recordings

A record run saves to the player's cards, its recording carries the saves it started from, and its replays run on a memory store seeded from them, never the player's (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 step 9f2c):

- **The record run** opens the player's store on `user:saves` and binds the cards' settings as any window run does, so the game reads and writes the player's cards and the recording holds the slots and their changes. `sl_input_record_start` is given the store, and the recording's `SAVE` chunk holds each save in it as the store held it at the start: a card's header save, `card-a`, and each of its files, `card-a-f000` on.
- **`--date <YYYY-MM-DD>T<HH:MM:SS>`** starts the console's clock at that local date and time instead of the host's, from 2000 on, so a record run starts on the date a test needs and its lines are the same from run to run.
- **A recording without `--ticks`** ends when the window closes ([Recording](#recording)), and a device script's `<retrace> close` closes it as a player does, at that tick's boundary.
- **A replay** of a recording that carries saves, even none, headless or in a window, opens a memory store with two backups, seeds it from them (`sl_input_replay_import_saves`) before `CARDInit` and binds the cards' settings, so the recorded slots apply through the replay and the game finds the cards the record run started from. The store ends with the run, and nothing writes its cards out.
- **The saves line.** A record run and a replay with a store print `ac_port: saves <n> hash <hash>` after their record or replay line: the store's save count, and `sl_hash64` over each save's name, with its terminating zero, and its container bytes (`sl_save_export`), in `sl_save_list`'s order, each seeded with the hash before, in 16 hex digits. A record run's and its replay's are the same when their cards end the same, byte for byte, sequences and save times included: a card file's save time is the card's virtual clock's, which a replay at the recorded calendar gives again.
- **A script's queue.** A device script's steps are all queued at its load, so a run with `--input-script` starts input with room for 65536 events, the library's default being 4096.

### The window

`--window` starts os and the runtime in normal mode on the host's own clock and date (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 steps 6k and 11a); with `--replay` they start as headless ([Replays](#replays)), and with `--record` deterministic from the host's date ([Recording](#recording)). The main thread sets up in this order:

1. sonarlib, then fs with the player's user directories: `user:` and `config:`, org `sonarlib` and app `animal-crossing`, on Linux `~/.local/share/sonarlib/animal-crossing` and `~/.config/sonarlib/animal-crossing`, or with `--user-dir <dir>` every one under `dir`, `config:` at `dir/config`. fs comes before the render module, so the GPU sink's pipeline list is `pipelines.slpc` in the cache directory, `~/.cache/sonarlib/animal-crossing` on Linux or `dir/cache` (sonarlib's `docs/rendering.md`, Pipeline list). The window run ends with `_Exit` and never reaches the render module's shutdown, so the list it keeps is the one the sink writes while it runs: once a list lacks a pipeline made and 120 frames in a row made none, about once a scene, on a thread of the sink's own. The next run's render module makes every pipeline the list names before the first frame;
2. the memory cards' save store (`sl_save_store_open`) on `user:saves`, `~/.local/share/sonarlib/animal-crossing/saves` on Linux or `dir/saves`, keeping two backups of each save, which it gives to the card glue (`ac_card_set_store`) for `CARDInit` to keep the cards in ([Saves](#saves)), where a replay opens none or the memory store its recording's saves seed ([Saves in recordings](#saves-in-recordings)); then the settings: it prints `ac_port: settings <host path>`, loads `config:settings.toml`, where no file is a first run, takes each `--set` over it for the session (`sl_settings_override`), adds a watch that marks the settings changed from whichever thread applies a change, and binds os's system settings (`sl_os_settings_bind`), so the game boots with the stored system settings. A file that does not load prints `ac_port: <host path>:<line>:<column>: <message>; it is left as it is` to stderr, or without the place when the error has none, and the run goes on at the defaults and never saves it;
3. the window system, then the render module on the GPU sink at 640x480, so its device shares the window system's video driver, then emu64's kit;
4. `game:`, the package; os; the game's sound on the host's audio device;
5. input, with the GameCube profile's default bindings: the keyboard on port 0, and each gamepad on the first free port as it arrives;
6. the window, `Animal Crossing`, 1280x960 and paced by VSync, then the binds that apply the settings in effect to it at once: its mode, pacing and limiter (`sl_window_settings_bind`), which also registers the picture's settings, `sonar.video.widescreen`, `sonar.video.render_scale` and the rest, and has the GPU sink's main target and post chain follow the window from its first frame (below), the volume (`sl_audio_settings_bind`) and the rumble strength (`sl_input_settings_bind`); then the card in each slot (`sl_memcard_settings_bind("card-a", NULL)`), `kit.card.slot_a` with `card-a` by default and `kit.card.slot_b` with none, which `CARDInit` puts in and a change swaps at the tick boundary. Every setting a `--set` can name is registered then, so each must name one that holds its override (`sl_settings_is_overridden`): one naming a key nothing registers, or with a value its setting refused at registration, prints `ac_port: --set <assignment>: <why>` and the usage to stderr and exits 2;
7. the overlay (`sl_ui_init`), F1 its toggle, with an `Animal Crossing` page: a Controls section with the keys, the gamepads, F1, and F11 or Alt+Enter, a Files section with the settings file's, the user directory's and the saves directory's host paths, and a Memory cards section with the two slots' settings, which edit them. Their widgets mark them as changing the simulation, since a slot's change applies at the tick boundary. sonarlib's Settings page lists only `sonar.*` keys, so this page is where the player changes them. A page's text is copied when it is added, so the page cannot show that a slot was left empty for a name the kit refused; that waits for the overlay's Saves page;
8. the game thread.

Each pass the main thread then pumps the window, which hands each event to the fullscreen keys, then the overlay, then input, and updates input; asks for each `--resize` whose tick has begun (below); begins the presented frame, which applies the video settings changed since the last and records the window's size in pixels for the main target to follow; presents the latest submitted frame at the window's alpha for the image's presentation time, the time `sl_window_frame_begin` gives (`sl_window_alpha`): the clock's (`sl_clock_alpha`), so the image blends the game's last two frames as the display's time falls between them, or 1 with `sonar.video.interpolation` off, when `sl_window_frame_begin` waits for each frame and the window presents it once, as the console showed it, and for a stale window; builds the overlay over it (`sl_ui_frame`) with the window's size and pixel density, the time since the last frame, the window and the frame resolved; and shows it, until the window is closed. Since sonarlib's step 9b each moving thing the title demo draws has an identity of its own and its jumps are marked, and the measured runs find no snap unexplained ([Measured runs](#measured-runs)); before it the window presented at 1. Each pass, when a setting changed since the last save and the overlay is hidden, it saves the file (`sl_settings_save_file`), which writes the stored values anew in tables, without the file's comments. The play line prints at play's first frame and the run goes on. `--ticks` applies only when given, and then ends the run once the runtime has begun that many ticks. At the end it saves them once more when a setting changed since the last save, unless the file did not load, a failure printing `ac_port: cannot save settings: <result>`. So a run that changes no setting leaves the file byte for byte as it was, comments too; `--set` changes none, as an override is for the session. Then it prints

```
ac_port: window ticks <t> frames <n> blended <n> audio <n> loud <n> dsp <n> pacing <vsync|mailbox|immediate|limiter> limit <Hz> gain <gain> pipelines <n> warmed <n> lists <n> cards <A> <B> target <width>x<height>
```

with the ticks begun, the frames presented and those of them blended, at an alpha below 1, the mix statistics and the DSP's sounding frames; the window's pacing and its limiter's rate in effect, from `sl_window_info`, in whole Hz, 0 for none; the audio device's gain (`sl_audio_output_gain`), (volume / 100)², to three decimals; and from `sl_render_stats`, the pipelines the GPU sink made, those of them it made from the pipeline list at the start, and the lists it wrote; then the card in each slot (`sl_memcard_get_card`), `-` for none, and the main target's size in pixels after the last present (`sl_render_get_target_size`). It exits 0 if play's first frame was reached or the window was closed, else 1. The game's sound plays on the host's audio device, on the driver the environment names, else the platform's. `--validate` and `--diff` work with it as without. Every other run mounts no user directory, reads no settings file, registers no setting but a replay's os settings, and puts no card in either slot, and `--user-dir`, `--set` or `--resize` without `--window` exits 2, as does a malformed `--set` at once, naming it, or a bad one after the binds.

**The picture follows the window** (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 step 9c1, and `docs/rendering.md`, The window followed). The port makes no call for it: the bind above is all, and each present makes the main target again when the window's size in pixels or one of the two settings changed. With `sonar.video.widescreen` on, its default, the target takes the window's aspect ratio, 640x480 widened by an even number of logical columns, so 854x480 in a 16:9 window; off, it keeps 640x480 with bars at the sides. With `sonar.video.render_scale` at 0, its default, the scale is the smallest whole one that covers the window, 2 in the 1280x960 window, so the first target is 1280x960 pixels; 1 to 16 fix it, 1 being the console's resolution. Neither changes what the game simulates, so a replay may set both and the state hashes are the same at every size. The render description (`sl_render_desc`) stays without a scale or an aspect, which hold only until the window's first frame.

**The picture's settings** (sonarlib's `docs/steps/m6-9e1.md`, M6 step 9e1, and `docs/rendering.md`, Post-processing and upscaling). The port makes no call and no edit for these either. - **Bad resizes.** `--ticks 60` with `--resize 10` and each of the sizes `640`, `640x`, `0x480` and `640x480x2`, with two resizes at tick 20, and with nine, must each exit 2 with the usage.
- **A goldens display's arguments** ([Goldens](#goldens)), with no `--window`: `--display 854x480` without `--goldens`; `--goldens` with `--display 854`, no size; `--goldens` with `--set sonar.video.widescreen=false` and no `--display`, which binds no setting; and `--goldens` with `--display 854x480 --set sonar.input.none=1`, a key the display's video settings do not register, must each exit 2 with the usage. The scenes file is one line the test writes, and no run reaches a frame.
- **The memory cards.** With the user directory emptied again, so no settings file, it runs `--ticks 900`, past play's first frame and so past `CARDInit`. The window line must read `cards card-a -` and the saves directory hold `card-a.slsave`, the card's header save. A second run with `--set kit.card.slot_b=card-b` must read `cards card-a card-b`, and the directory hold `card-b.slsave`. A settings file either run writes must hold no `kit.card` line, as the defaults never changed and an override is never saved. Their exit status is not checked.

It is skipped without a disc or lavapipe. In real time the play line differs between runs.

The GPU sink compiles pipelines on the game thread, and the GPU driver's shader compiler can be C++ (lavapipe's LLVM is): its allocations then reach the game's `operator new`. sonarlib runs every render call inside a host scope (`sl_mem_host_scope`), and `JKRHeap.cpp`'s rule sends those allocations to the host's allocator, never JKR's heaps.

### Sound

Every run starts the game's sound: `ac_port.c` calls `ac_audio_init` after os starts, on sonarlib's device sink under `--window` and its null sink headless. Animal Crossing's music and sounds come from jaudio_NES's sequenced engine, the N64-style `Nas_*` engine, which mixes on the CPU (`rspsim.c`) into the blocks the AI glue (`adapter/dolphin_ai.c`) plays. The boot jingle is JAudio1's ([Boot sound](#boot-sound)), and the NES emulator's sound plays only in the NES games.

The engine reads `audiorom.img`: sequences, then banks, then waves, as the game's compiled tables (`game/audioheaders.c`) give them. It copies a bank into its cache and turns the bank's offsets into pointers in place (`Nas_BankOfsToAddr_Inner`, `__WaveTouch`), so the banks must be the host's structures. The importer (`importer/audio.cpp`) turns the game's tables into entries for sonarlib's `kits/jaudio` (`sonar/jaudio/nas_rom.h`), which lays every bank out again at the host's sizes, 32-byte aligned, with each structure at a multiple of 16 and its pointers as offsets in the bank; the sequence and wave regions stay the disc's bytes, the waves moved by what the banks grew. On `GAFE01_00` the banks grow from 0x67C80 to 0x8F300 bytes. `importer/audio.cpp` checks each kit structure against the game's own (`static_assert`), and gives the kit this build's limits: `NA_RELOCATED_BASE`, the caches' room below and at most 0x40000 of growth. `audiorom.tbl` holds the host image's table entries, host-order `u32`s after a version word and the two counts; `Nas_InitAudio` calls `ac_audio_rom_tables` (`dolphin_dvd.c`) to write them into `AudiobankHeaderStart` and `AudiodataHeaderStart` before it reads them, and a package without it stops the run with a message.

Sequences and envelopes stay the console's bytes: a sequence is a byte stream whose tables are found only by running it, and sequences hold envelopes too. So the engine reads their 16-bit values with `sl_load_be16` where it reads them: the sequence map (`system.c`, `track.c`), a sequence's `u16` operands (`track.c`), `melody.c`'s patch of a sequence's offsets (with `sl_store_be16`), and `Nas_EnvInit` (`effect.c`); `DEFAULT_ENV` (`audiotable.c`) is written in the console's byte order. Sample data is read a byte at a time. Loops and books come only from banks, and the kit converts them.

The pools at host sizes: `Jac_Start`'s ARAM is 0x850000 (`game64.c_inc`), a persistent bank's cache 0x6000 and the temporary cache 0x3400, two slots of 0x1A00 (`audioconst.c`), and jaudio's heap 0x22C00 larger in both places that size it (`neosthread.c`, `game64.c_inc`'s `audiomemory`), so the misc heap keeps its room. Measured at tick 3600 of a window run on `GAFE01_00`: misc 0x42E20 of 0x49400, emem 0x19B60 of 0x28000, the temporary bank cache 0x13E0 (one bank) of 0x3400, the persistent cache empty. The largest host banks: a permanent one 0x6AA0, a temporary one 0x19C0, a persistent one 0x5D20.

The test `ac_import_disc` checks both entries against the disc through the game's own tables and structures: the table's counts, each data entry where the growth puts it and the regions around the banks byte for byte, and in every bank each structure the engine relocates, followed from the host head by its pointers and compared field by field with the disc's, read big-endian.

### Boot sound

The jingle at boot (`BootSound`, `game64_dummy.c_inc`) is JAudio1's, the engine other GameCube games share: a sequence (`NIN_SEQ`), a bank (`NIN_BANK`, an IBNK) and a wave set (`NIN_WAVE`, a WSYS), which the executable keeps. The sequence is a byte stream and stays the console's. JAudio1 reads the bank and the wave set in place, as structures of 32-bit offsets it turns into pointers (`Bank_Test`, `Wave_Test`, with `PTconvert`) and integers in the console's order, so the build lays them out for the host. The decompilation's build extracts both (`config.yml`, `assets/jaudio/NIN_BANK.bin` and `NIN_WAVE.bin`), and `ac-boot-sound` (`importer/boot_sound.cpp`) lays each out with sonarlib's jaudio kit (`sonar/jaudio/jaudio1.h`) and writes `boot_sound.inc`, which `game64_dummy.c_inc` includes in place of the two arrays. Each structure is the host's, at a multiple of 16 in the console's order, with its relocated pointers as offsets in the image, which the engine's `PTconvert` turns into pointers; the others keep the console's value. The tool checks each kit structure against the game's own (`bx.h`, `heapctrl.h`, `static_assert`): `Vmap_` as the code reads it, a `u32` of the wave set's number and the wave's at 4 (`map[1]`, `oneshot.c`), where `bx.h`'s two `s16` fall at 2 and 4, and `Wave_`'s declared fields, after which the kit keeps the console's word at 0x28. The layout is the building machine's, like `ac-font`'s. On `GAFE01_00` the bank grows from 0x880 to 0xE40 bytes and the wave set from 0x240 to 0x2A0. `BootSound` then registers both as on the console, and the jingle plays on the DSP glue's channels (`adapter/dolphin_dsp.c`, on the kit's microcode), which count the frames in which a rendered sample reaches 1/256 of full scale: the proof's `dsp`.

On the jingle's path the engine reads a sound id's bytes as bank, program, key and velocity, so `noteon.c` and `jammain_2.c` store its value with `sl_store_be32` and `oneshot.c` loads it with `sl_load_be32`; `Jam_SeqmainNote` (`jammain_2.c`) reads a track's children and `_D5` by name rather than by console offsets; `EffecterInit_Perc` (`oneshot.c`) reads a percussion key map's two `Rand_` pointers by name; and `FORCE_RELEASE_TABLE` (`bankdrv.c`) is the six `s16` the console reads.

The test `ac_boot_sound` lays out the two extracts again, walks each image through the game's structures as `Bank_Test` and `Wave_Test` reach them, compares every field with the console's bytes, read big-endian, every relocated pointer with the place of the structure the console's offset names and every other pointer with the console's value, and checks that the build's `boot_sound.inc` is what the tool writes. It fails on an instrument or a voice, which the jingle has none of.

### Saves

With `--window` the memory cards are kept in the save store on `user:saves` ([The window](#the-window); `README.md`, Saves), through sonarlib's `kits/card` (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 steps 11a to 11c). A record run keeps them there too, and a replay of a recording that carries saves keeps them on a memory store seeded from it ([Saves in recordings](#saves-in-recordings)). The store holds each file in the console's bytes, as a GameCube card does, and the game reads and writes the kit's host copy of it, in the host's layout. Three views in `adapter/ac_saves.c`, which `CARDInit` hands the kit, convert between the two for the game's three kinds of file: `DobutsunomoriP_MURA`, the town under both its names (`m_card.c:1526-1527`), so its rename keeps the host copy; `DobutsunomoriP_PL_`, the passports; and `DobutsunomoriP_Omake_`, the gift letters. `adapter/ac_saves.schema` lays out the structures they hold, written by hand from the game's headers with every structure `same`, and `sonar_add_schema` generates their converters. The test `ac_save_schema_check` checks every structure against the port's DWARF, with the event areas' `data` named as the port's. `mCD_foreigner_c` (`m_card.c:1844-1850`) and `aESC_event_save_c` (`ac_ev_soncho.c:5-8`) are defined in the game's `.c` files, so `ac_port.h` repeats them for the views and their test under `AC_SAVES_TYPES`.

**The file maps.** Each file starts with the card header, `MemcardHeader_c`: the comment, the banner and the icons, `[0, 0x1440)`, copied as they are both ways. The banner and icons are the console's texels and palettes, as `ac-import` keeps them ([The archives](#the-archives)). After it, each structure converts by its offset, whatever it holds: a region with a bad checksum, an erased one or one of zeros converts the same way, so the game reads it as it did on the console.

The kit's host copy starts on a multiple of 32 bytes (sonarlib's `SL_MEMCARD_HOST_ALIGN`), as the card library's buffers did, and each offset above is a multiple of its structure's alignment, 32 at most (`ATTRIBUTE_ALIGN`), so each structure converts in place in the copy. `ac_saves.c` asserts both.

**The protect code.** The town's header, `[0, 0x1460)`, is bytes except the four `u16` at 0x1440, which `mCD_CheckProtectCode` reads natively (`m_card.c:1907-1927`, `2286`): 0x3C1C, then three words that are not 0. No C type lays the header out, so `ac_saves.c` converts the four words itself.

**The keep regions' padding.** A keep structure's `sizeof` is the same on both (`same` asserts it), and the game sums and copies it to its size aligned to 32 (`l_aram_real_size_32_table`, `m_card.c:1563-1567`). The bytes from the structure's end to there, 0xBAA4 to 0xBAC0 for keep_mail and 0xBA02 to 0xBA20 for keep_diary, are padding that the views copy as it is, and the checksum covers it.

**What converts.** Every integer converts by its width: `u64` and `OSTime` are 8 bytes, `f32` 4. `char` text and `u8` arrays are bytes. Bit-fields are `bits` lines over their console containers, the two signed ones with `s`; `mQst_base_c`'s container is 16 bits, and `bridge_flags` is bytes. A hole inside a structure reads as zero on the host, as `from_console` leaves it, and keeps its console bytes on the card, as `update_console` does; the checksums' carry covers both.

**The unions and their rules.**

- The errand info is `union ... by errand_type`: `case 0,1` is the chain arm and `case 2` the first-job arm, with no `else`. Another value of the signed 3-bit field, one the game never sets, copies the union's bytes as they are. The chain arm covers the prefix both arms share, so an errand NONE (0) that the game later makes a chain or a first job keeps its ids, and zeros convert to zeros. A case and an `else` cannot both name the chain arm, since `sl-schema` refuses two lines naming one member.
- The contest info is always the letter arm, and the weekly event always `kabu_peddler`.
- The special event is `by kind`, cases 0 (SHOP) to 5 (GYPSY). None, 0xFFFFFFFF, has no case, so its zeros are copied.
- The villager memories: the schema has two of `Animal_c` and of `Anmmem_c`, the land arm for `animals[15]` and the passport's `remove_animal`, and the island arm for `island.animal`.
- `leaflet_recipient_flags` is its two `s16`.

**The event save areas.** `mEv_save_common_data_c` holds five areas, each `info` and 44 bytes of `data`. The generated converter converts `info` and leaves `data` to `ac_saves.c`, which converts it after: for each area whose `area_use_bitfield` bit is set, by the structure `(info.type, info.id)` picks; for an area whose bit is clear, as its bytes. Both directions read the bit and the pair from the host's structure, so one value picks one layout both ways. A pair no layout names converts as `int[11]`, logged once a run. A new town file is 0xFF in every byte (sonarlib's `memcard.cpp`), so its first host copy, before the game writes the town, shows each area in use as (255, 255). No area of the game's has that pair: `mEv_reserve_save_area` stores the type it is given (`m_event.c:2267-2301`), and each type the game passes is below `mEv_EVENT_NUM` (119). It converts as `int[11]`, as any pair no layout names, and is not logged. Each structure is one of `ac_saves.schema` that no top structure reaches, and its members convert by their types, `PersonalID_c` and `lbRTC_ymd_c` by their fields; `ac_saves.c` asserts each fits in the 44 bytes. The bytes past a structure keep the console's.

Every other cast reads a member of the structure the table names, at its offset and in its type, so one layout serves them all. The summer camper's area is read through a pointer to an `mActor_name_t`, and no structure of the game lays it out.

**The checksums** carry the console's sum, never 0. `to_host` computes S, the big-endian sum of the console's region, converts, then sets the host's checksum so the host's native sum is S. `to_console` computes H, the native sum of the host's region, converts with `update_console`, then sets the console's checksum so the big-endian sum is H. So a valid region is valid both ways, a bad one stays bad by the same amount, an erased one keeps its non-zero sum, and a round trip gives the console's checksum again. The game's sums are `mFRm_ReturnCheckSum`'s, the `u16` words added natively (`m_flashrom.c:110-120`).

**The dates.** The schemas convert `mEv_MonthDay_u` as its `u16` arm, never as its bytes. On the host that arm holds the console's number, month above day ([The edits on the path](#the-edits-on-the-path), `m_event.h`), so a byte swap of the number gives the console's bytes, month first: December 25 is `0C 19` on the card. A saved type that came to hold `mEv_schedule_date_u` or `mEv_ymdh_u` would convert its `u32` arm the same way. `lbRTC_time_c` converts its year as a `u16` and the rest as bytes.

**The keep diary's overrun.** When the player travels, `mCD_SaveStation_NextLand_load_others` copies the other town's keep_original, 0xCCA0 bytes, into `l_keepDiary`, where it means `l_keepOriginal` (the decompilation's `@BUG`). On the console the union is 0xC000 bytes, and the copy runs 0xCA0 bytes past it. Under `SONAR_PORT` its buffer is `mCD_ALIGN_SECTORSIZE(sizeof(mCD_keep_original_c))`, 0xE000 bytes, so the copy stays inside it. What the console's bytes past the union were overwritten with is not reproduced.

**The files no view covers** are their bytes both ways: the Famicom (NES) games' saves and the NES ROMs a card can hold. That is right for the ROMs, and for the saves' checksum, a sum of bytes (`calcSum`, `famicom.cpp:157`). It is not right for the rest of a save: its header, at the file's data offset (`famicom.cpp:1113`), holds a `u16` size (`famicom.h:73`), and the game sets the first-play bits in a native `u32` at the save data's +4 (`famicom_nesinfo.cpp:741-742`). The port writes those in the host's order, so a card's NES saves would not read on a console. The Famicom emulator does not start in the port yet ([What runs silent or does nothing](#what-runs-silent-or-does-nothing)). Gift letter files from outside the game and the Game Boy Advance link are not handled either.

**The timing.** The game writes a town save a sector at a time (`mCD_write_comp_bg_read`, `m_card.c:602-630`): it reads each sector and writes those that differ, and each write converts the whole 0x72000-byte host copy to the console's layout. `ac_card_port` times a new town's save that way, its 57 sectors each read and then written through `CARDRead` and `CARDWrite` on a memory store, and one whole-town conversion each way. Over five runs of the RelWithDebInfo build: the reads 0.30 to 1.26 ms in all, the writes 14.0 to 17.2 ms in all, the store's writes among them; one town to the host 0.18 to 0.23 ms and to the console 0.04 ms. So a save's 57 conversions take about 2.3 ms.

The test `ac_save_views` works each case by hand in the console's bytes: a field of each kind, each kind of bit-field container, each union rule, the event areas, the dates, the protect code, the keep regions' padding, the checksums of each region kind, and 64 random files of each kind to the host and back, which must be their bytes again.

### The reference's program

The build makes two programs of the same objects (sonarlib's roadmap.md, M6 step 10a). `ac_port`, the player's, is `adapter/ac_port.c`, the game and the glue (`ac_game`), the saves (`ac_saves`) and `adapter/reference_none.c`. `ac_port_reference`, a test program, is the same less `reference_none.c`, with the reference (`ac_reference`) in its place: the console's emu64 (`src/static/libforest/emu64/emu64.c`, under the `emu64_ref_` names `port/CMakeLists.txt` gives it), `adapter/emu64_reference.c`, the glue's half that runs it, `adapter/gx_reference.c`, the GX front ends it draws through, and `adapter/jsys_display_reference.c`, the GX of the frame's seams that it draws under (sonarlib's step 10e).

- **The hooks.** The shared files reach the reference only through `adapter/include/port/reference.h`, which `reference_none.c` defines inert and the reference's three files define for real: the two flags' enables, the boot's buffers, the emu64 entry points' calls kept for the reference, the task, the report's field and the diff line, the GX hand-over and record to the reference's front ends, and the frame seams' waits, flushes and display copy. `adapter/include/port/display_reference.h` adds the frame seams' hooks that name GX's types: the copy's set-up, `preGX`'s state and `clearEfb`'s quad. Only `emu64_reference.c` and `gx_reference.c` include `adapter/emu64_reference.h`, which declares emu64.c's functions, the reference's front end and the buffers `OSInit` takes for it, so a shared file that called one would fail `ac_port`'s link.
- **The options.** `ac_port` refuses `--diff`, `--diff-from` and `--reference`, exit 2 with its usage, which names none of them; `ac_port_reference`'s usage names them, `usage: ac_port_reference --package <file> [--ticks <n>] [--validate] [--diff [--diff-from <tick>]] [--reference]`, its other lines indented to that name. `--diff-from` without `--diff`, or given twice, exits 2. Every other option is the same in both.
- **One game.** Without either flag `ac_port_reference` plays as `ac_port`: each hook does nothing, the kit draws, and its play line is `ac_port`'s byte for byte, which `ac_port_measure` checks ([Measured runs](#measured-runs)). emu64.c's variables are in `ac_port_reference` alone, so `ac_port`'s symbol manifest no longer names them ([State hashes](#state-hashes)); the game's state hashes are unchanged.
- **No GX front end in `ac_port`.** Since sonarlib's step 10e the main front end, which takes the GX written outside display lists, is `gx_reference.c`'s, made only under `--diff` or `--reference` and with `SL_GXF_NO_CLEAR`, as the frame's clear is the jsys kit's in both programs ([The glue](#the-glue)). Under either flag the frame's seams make the game's GX calls through the hooks, so the front end reads the stream the console's GX did; without them the hooks do nothing and nothing creates it. `ac_port` links no `gx-frontend`: the test `ac_port_front_end` lists `ac_port`'s symbols with the toolchain's `nm`, which must hold the jsys kit's `sl_jsys_display_clear` and nothing that starts with `sl_gxf_`, and prints `ac_port_front_end: no sl_gxf_ symbol`. It needs no disc.

### The emu64 differential test

`ac_port_reference --diff` draws every emu64 task twice from the same game state, through the kit and through the console's emu64 (`src/static/libforest/emu64/emu64.c`, built into `ac_port_reference` as the reference, [The reference's program](#the-references-program)), and compares the two (sonarlib's validation.md, Differential tests, and roadmap.md, M6 step 6j). The reference draws through the SDK's GX units into a second GX front end. Each of the two goes into a frame of its own with captures on, the kit's first, and the game's frame begins again after them for the task's fader and 2D, so a `--diff` tick submits four frames. Without the flag none of the reference runs and no frame is captured, unless `--reference` draws every frame with it ([Measured runs](#measured-runs)). A task whose frame or capture is refused, or in which the reference's palettes overflowed, counts as refused and is not compared. A task is equal when its two captures compare and hold as many copies of the frame (`sl_capture.copies`, sonarlib's step 10b). The kit's draws are labeled by the command that drew them, which the glue names as a field of the task's lists and its index (`polygon_opaque 412`), as `static` and its GX address, or as `unknown`. For each task that differs it prints, at most 16 times a run, the first difference in its captures, or, when they compare, the copies each holds:

```
ac_port: diff tick <tick> triangle <t> vertex <v> field <field> index <i> kit <draw> <name> reference <draw>
ac_port: diff tick <tick> copies kit <n> reference <n>
```

and at the end, before the play line, the tasks compared, the triangles of the equal tasks summed over both captures, the draws each skipped, and the kit's copies in the equal tasks:

```
ac_port: diff tasks <n> equal <n> different <n> refused <n> triangles <n> skipped <n> <n> copies <n>
```

`--diff` also goes with a headless `--replay`: the diff line comes at play's first frame, before the play line, and again at the replay's end, before the replay line, each for the tasks since the run began. `--diff-from <tick>` still draws and compares every task before that tick, so the reference's state follows the game's, but counts and prints only the tasks from it on.

The test `ac_port_diff` imports the disc and runs `ac_port_reference --diff`, which must reach play's first frame and exit 0 with tasks compared, none different, none refused and `copies 0`; it prints the differences, the diff line and the play line, and is skipped without a disc. ### Goldens

`--goldens <scenes> <dir>` writes the frames a scenes file names, for comparison with Dolphin's (sonarlib's validation.md, Port goldens, and roadmap.md, M6 step 6n). The run is headless and deterministic, but on sonarlib's GPU sink with no window, and play's first frame does not end it. At each frame's begin the GX glue hands sonarlib's `sl_goldens_frame` the frame submitted before it, with its keys as they stand then, after that frame's tick, which is where Dolphin's MemoryWatcher reads them, and the part of the frame buffer the VI shows, which the VI glue passes to `kit_vi` from `VIConfigure` and `VIConfigurePan`. ### Measured runs

`--measure` times the title demo, or with `--replay` a recording's play, from play's first frame to the tick limit, frame by frame (sonarlib's validation.md, Performance budgets, Measured runs, and roadmap.md, M6 steps 7b, 9a, 9b and 9f3b). The run is headless and deterministic, as a plain run is, but on sonarlib's GPU sink with no window, and play's first frame does not end it. The process's main thread opens sonarlib's `sonar/measure.h` with the tick limit and the present rate, and serves. From the frame that holds play's first task, the GX glue's `ac_frame_end` calls `ac_measure_frame` after its submit, which hands the frame to `sl_measure_frame` and waits while the main thread presents it: once, at alpha 1, or with `--present-hz <n>` at each of a display's times at `n` Hz in the frame's tick, at the clock's alpha. ```

    storage <n> pushes <n>
ac_port: presents <hz> count <n> held <n> late <n> dropped <n> backward <n> pairs <n> unexplained <n> none <n>

the first two each on one line. #### The lists' room

### State hashes

`--state-hashes <file>` writes a hash of the game's state at each tick boundary, and `--dump <dir> --dump-tick <n>` dumps the state at one, for sonarlib's `sl-dump-compare` to name what differs (sonarlib's `docs/roadmap.md`, M6 step 9f3b, and `docs/platform.md`, State hashes and State dumps):

```sh
port/build/ac_port --package ac.pkg --replay town.slir --state-hashes hashes.txt --dump dumps --dump-tick 20000
port/build/sonarlib/tools/sl-dump-compare --symbols port/build/ac_port.slsym dumps/tick20000.sldump other/tick20000.sldump
```

- **What is hashed.** With `--state-hashes`, `adapter/ac_state.c` registers five entries with sonarlib's `sonar/state.h` and sets the listener before the runtime starts, so the first boundary hashes them; a run without it hashes nothing. In order:
  - `common_data`: the save's bytes, padding and all, as the card holds them, then each top-level field after the save by its `offsetof` and `sizeof` on the host, leaving out those that are or hold pointers, `clip`, `now_private`, `now_home`, `npc_schedule`, `start_demo_request`, `pluss_bridge_pos` and `carde_program_p`, and the gaps between the fields. Padding nested in a field hashed as its bytes is hashed, as the game's code left it; another compiler may leave it otherwise, which the hash matrix across compilers would find.
  - `qrand_idum` and `qrand_itemp`: qrand's two words, through `qrand_state` ([The edits on the path](#the-edits-on-the-path)).
  - `game_clocks`: while `graph_proc` has a game (`game_class_p`), its `frame_counter`, and while that game is play, whose `exec` is `play_main`, its `game_frame`.
  - `actors`: while the game is play, each of `actor_info`'s eight lists in order, its count, then each actor's fields from `id` to `ground_y`, field by field, in list order.

  The audio's state, the emulator's and the adapter's are left out; the replay line checks the audio's ([Replays](#replays)). With sonarlib's step 10e no hash changed: built before the step and with it, the hash files of `ac_port_state` and `ac_port_state_measured` were the same byte for byte, and each build's headless replay of the other's title recording gave the other's record run's hashes. The record runs' own files differ from build to build, as each recording holds the calendar it was made on: the two parted at tick 490.
- **The file.** A host path, replaced, with a line for each boundary, `<tick> <hash>`, the tick in decimal and the hash in 16 hex digits, which the listener writes and flushes, as runs end with `_Exit`. The hash at tick n is the state the game left at the end of tick n - 1, which a dump for tick n holds. A headless replay ends at a retrace, before its last tick's boundary, so the town replay's file has ticks 0 to 23999, and a measured run's file ends where the run does. A window's replay and a record run end in the present loop, after the retrace callback has seen their end, and the boundaries go on until the loop stops, so their files are longer than a headless replay's by a few lines, as many as the host's timing gives, and the headless replay's file is their prefix.
- **Dumps.** `--dump <dir>` mounts `<dir>` writable as `dumps:`, created when missing, and requests `dumps:tick<n>.sldump` for `--dump-tick <n>`, as sonarlib's tick30 example does. The dump holds the state the hash at tick n covers, and more: every variable the symbol manifest keeps, main memory as an arena, and the game threads' stacks' ranges, so a pointer into one compares by thread and offset.
- **Where.** Both are allowed in every deterministic run: headless, measured, and a window's `--replay` or `--record`. `--state-hashes` or `--dump` in a window without either, `--dump` without `--dump-tick` or the reverse, and `--plant` without `--state-hashes` exit 2 with the usage and write no file.

### The glue

- **DVD** (`adapter/dolphin_dvd.c`): the package, listed once into a table sorted by path; an entry number is the index. Paths are relative to the disc's root, without regard to case. Reads complete at once. Directory listing and streaming fail.
- **OS** (`adapter/dolphin_os.c`): `OSInit` binds boot's main thread, the game thread `main`, as the SDK's at priority 16. It is the runtime's tick thread throughout: its retrace waits begin ticks, and while it waits in os the runtime moves time.
- **VI and PAD** (`adapter/dolphin_vi.c`, `dolphin_pad.c`) on sonarlib's `kit_vi` and `kit_pad`. Frames pace on `JUTVideo`'s retrace queue through the VI glue's post-retrace callback, as on the console; `setFrameRate` also sets the tick's length in retraces. `VIConfigure` sets the kit's pan to the whole frame buffer and `VIConfigurePan` to its rectangle, as the SDK's do.
- **GX** (`adapter/dolphin_gx.c`, `include/port/gx_sink.h`): the SDK's own GX units are compiled for the host, writing into a sink in the console's byte order. Outside a display list the bytes are handed over at each `GXFlush`, at the frame's end and before each record (sonarlib's step 10e): on a thread that is not a game thread they are dropped as an error; under `--diff` or `--reference` they go to the reference's front ends ([The reference's program](#the-references-program)); and otherwise `GXInit`'s, written in the glue's init bracket (`ac_gx_init`, which `JUTGraphFifo`'s seam calls in its place), are dropped, as are bytes all zero, `GXFlush`'s, and any other byte is an error, as no seam writes GX there. Display lists fill their buffers as on the console. `JW_BeginFrame` begins the frame and `JW_EndFrame` records the last bytes and submits it. Pointers written as 32-bit values become GX addresses: main memory at 0, then the executable's read-only and initialized data ranges, each 32-byte aligned after the one before, within the 512 MiB that the registers' 24-bit fields reach; NULL is 0, and a pointer in no region is 0 and counted as unmapped. `OSInit` lays the space out and logs its table. `GXFifo.c` is not compiled: the glue keeps the FIFO objects, and the GPU is always idle, its draw-done and draw-sync complete at once. `__GXSaveCPUFifoAux` calls `GXFlush` first, as the SDK's does, so the flush's 32 zero bytes go into the FIFO being saved; for a display list the write pointer is then rounded down to 32 bytes from the list's base, as the console's moves by bursts, and the list's count runs from there (`copy_efb_to_texture`'s counts 64). Until step 10b the flush's bytes were left out and the count cut the list in the middle of a command. `ac_gx_memory` (`gx_sink.h`) gives the host bytes at a GX address, for the reference's front ends and the emu64 kit. The GX thread is the glue's (`GXSetCurrentGXThread`, `ac_gx_set_thread`): `emu64_init` sets it, and shutdown's seam reads it (the frame, below).
- **emu64** (`adapter/emu64_glue.c`, `include/port/emu64_glue.h`) on sonarlib's `kits/emu64`. `emu64_taskstart` records the task's lists with `sl_emu64_task` into the frame `JW_BeginFrame` opened, after the GX written before it, and the run ends when the frame holding play's first task is submitted. The task passes no clear: the frame's clear is `JFWDisplay`'s seam's, a view of its own that reaches a wider target's edges ([The window](#the-window)). `emu64_init` makes its caller the GX thread first, as the console's did (`emu64.c:699-701`): the graph thread each frame, and the boot path's two calls. Before step 10e the GX thread stayed the boot thread, which called `GXInit`, and shutdown's cancel would have read the wrong thread. `emu64_texture_cache_data_entry_set` and `emu64_refresh` go to the kit; at start the program adds the executable's read-only and initialized data as static ranges (`sl_mem_image_data`) and gives the kit GX's memory (`sl_emu64_set_memory` with `ac_gx_memory`). A task's GX list, `m_play.c`'s copy of the frame for a submenu's background, is the kit's: it decodes the list and records the copy into a target that the background's tile then binds (sonarlib's step 10b); at a wider display the copy takes the display's columns and the background's rectangle is stretched. Owners are actors: `Actor_draw` writes an owner marker at the head of each of `GRAPH`'s eleven display lists, `(key << 4) | (list + 1)` for the actor's key, takes back each marker the actor drew nothing after and closes the other lists; `Actor_dt` forgets the actor's owners. An actor's key is its offset in main memory, or, for an actor in a table of static actors, 2^27 + table × 2^8 + slot: `ac_emu64_actor_table` registers a table by its base, stride and count, an actor inside element k being slot k, and registering it again replaces it. Table 0 is the NPCs' nine buffers (`aNPC_n_actor_cl_tbl`, `aNPC_keep_actor_class`), table 1 the structures' (`aSTR_actor_cl`, the structure clip's setup). `dolphin_os.c` asserts main memory is at most 2^27 bytes, so offsets and table keys stay apart, the keys below 2^28 and the owners below 2^32, a marker's w1. An actor with no key, outside main memory and every table, gets no owner and counts in `unowned`. Parts are owners inside an actor's owner, for what one actor draws many of: `ac_emu64_part_open` and `ac_emu64_part_close` write and close a part marker in each of the eleven lists, taking back those the part drew nothing after, and `ac_emu64_part_open_at` and `ac_emu64_part_close_at` do it in the one list a draw holds open; a part's ids are `sl_emu64_part(owner, part)`'s, and a part opened in a part is the enclosing part's. The weather's particles, the effects, the field's blocks, the bg items' positions and pools, and the title logo's layers each draw in a part ([The edits on the path](#the-edits-on-the-path)), so a slot keeps its ids when others fill and empty. The game's jumps are marked: `ac_emu64_forget_part` forgets a part of the actor's owners when what fills its slot is new, `ac_emu64_snap_part` and `ac_emu64_snap_actor` snap a part or the actor in the next task's frame, and `ac_emu64_snap_all` the whole frame; `ac_emu64_actor_made` records up to 256 actors made, by key, after each `ct_proc`, and `ac_emu64_snap_made`, before `Actor_info_call_actor`'s moves, snaps their owners, or the whole frame past 256. Forgetting an actor's owners does not forget its parts, which are owners of their own; the sites that reuse a slot forget its part as they fill it. ### Open items

- **The title demo's position differences.** `ac_port_reference --diff --replay` on the town replay shows four position differences in the title's demo, at ticks 724, 831, 834 and 903, each in a static list drawn by the kit and the reference alike (`0x06f0a650`, `0x06f09a50`, `0x06f16770`). They come before any copy and are not step 10b's; `ac_port_diff`'s replay run starts after them, at `--diff-from 1000`. Their cause is a follow-up.
- **The noise at boot.** In `--window` the sound garbles for a moment after the Nintendo jingle and the train's sound. The DVD queue's fix does not explain it: with the title left idle to tick 3600, the runs' lines with and without the fix are the same, the sound's hash too.
- **GX's pixel center.** sonarlib draws with a pixel's center half a pixel in, and Dolphin puts GX's at 7/12 of a pixel. It shows in the debug text, whose glyphs land on whole pixels and are sampled bilinearly, by a twelfth of a texel at their edges ([Goldens](#goldens)). It is the library's, for any GameCube game; how much of the goldens' difference at the logo's edges it explains is not measured.
- **A made actor's parts.** A part's ids sit under `sl_emu64_part(owner, part)`, so `ac_emu64_forget_actor` and the snap of a made actor (`ac_emu64_snap_made`) reach the owner's ids but not its parts', as sonarlib's step 9b has it. An actor that draws in parts and is made at the main-memory offset of one that just died would blend its part n with the old actor's part n for one frame. None does today: every actor that draws in parts (the weather, the effect control, the bg items, the field and the title logo) is made once a scene, and `game_ct` snaps all. The fix belongs in the kit, which would keep the parts each owner opened and forget or snap them with it, in a later step.
